Day 11 · 27 November, 500 BCE

Thebes to Haliartos

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

Polykritos and Theano saw me off before sunrise and gave me bread for the road. Then I walked west out of Thebes across the plain. Mount Helikon was on my left all day, rising grey and high above the fields. For about 17 km the ground was flat, and I could see many men who were behind their ploughs. Near the end of that stretch the donkey and I climbed a little way up to Onchestos.

I had thought that Onchestos was a village, but it turned out to be a sanctuary of the god Poseidon. There was a sacred grove with a stone temple and a bigger hall beside it where people from the Boeotian towns met. Nobody lived there. Homer called it "Poseidon's bright grove", and in the low afternoon sun the leaves were bright. Somewhere in the hills to the south-west, less than 10 km away, was Askra, the village of Hesiod. Eight days ago, on the plain behind Eleusis, I heard the cranes and remembered his verse about ploughing. Now I was walking past the very fields Hesiod had written about.

From Onchestos I saw Lake Copais for the first time, a huge flat sheet of grey water and reeds reaching north farther than my eyes could follow. The town of Haliartos was about 4 km further on, and I got there at dusk, tired but happy to have made it. Kaphisias, a farmer of Haliartos who takes in travellers, gave me a bed, a bowl of hot beans and barley for the donkey. He took his pay in my new Theban silver.

Photo: Modern walkers climbing Mount Helikon in snow, photographed about 2008. Helikon is the mountain south of the road from Thebes to Haliartos. Arion saw it only from the road below. Photo Credit: Photo by Constantinos Gofas, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Based on evidence: From Thebes to Onchestos was 17 km on flat ground, and on the last section the road climbed toward Onchestos. Onchestos was about 4 km east of Haliartos. Homer called it "holy Onchestos, Poseidon's bright grove". A temple and a larger meeting hall were built there in the mid 500s BCE, and no village or town of the late 500s has been found there. Hesiod's village of Askra lay less than 10 km south-west of Onchestos.
