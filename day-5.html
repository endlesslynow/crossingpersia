Day 5 · 21 November, 500 BCE

Oinoe to Hysiai

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

All morning the donkey and I climbed up the valley from Oinoe toward Eleutherai, the last village below the top of the pass over Mount Cithaeron. The drizzle got colder with every bend. By the time we passed Eleutherai my cloak was heavy with water and my fingers had gone stiff on the donkey's rope. Near the top the rain turned into a wet cloud so thick that I could see the donkey's ears and not much else.

At the top of the pass the cloud tore open for a moment, and I saw Boeotia below me. In the valley of the river Asopos there were brown fields and dark woods. Beyond them, to the north, was the land of Thebes. There was no fort and no guard, nobody at all, only the wind and the two of us. I stood there shivering and grinning like a boy. Everything past that river is new to me.

Going down the north side of the mountain was steep and quick, and we reached the village of Hysiai at dusk, soaked to the skin. Nikarete, a widow who farms at the edge of Hysiai, saw the state of us and opened her gate before I could ask. The donkey went into her yard, my cloak went on a peg by the fire, and a bowl of hot barley porridge went into me. I was asleep before her sons had finished eating.

Photo: Mount Cithaeron (Kithaironas) seen from the south-east, the Attic side, photographed in August 2020. The wind turbines on the ridge are modern. Photo Credit: Photo by Matthiasberlin, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Based on evidence: From the plain of Oinoe, travellers went up to Eleutherai, the highest point of the road at 637 metres, and then down to Hysiai and the valley of the Asopos. Eleutherai and Oinoe both existed in the last quarter of the 500s BCE, and the forts built there later were only 6 km apart. The first fort at Eleutherai was built in the 400s BCE and replaced by a large fortress in the 300s.
