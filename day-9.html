Day 9 · 25 November, 500 BCE

Thebes

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

Polykritos took me down to the market of Thebes this morning to change money. My Athenian owls are too heavy a coin to hand over for a night's bed, and the few small coins I brought from home are nearly gone. Mnasippos, a money-changer of Thebes, sat behind a table with a pair of little bronze scales. I put one owl down in front of him. Mnasippos weighed it, turned it over to look at Athena's face, and weighed it again.

The Theban coins had the Boeotian shield on one side, an oval with a notch cut into each side. On the other side was the old letter Theta for Thebes, a small circle with a cross in it, in the middle of a pattern like the sails of a mill. Polykritos told me the other Boeotian towns put the same shield on their coins. With an owl in one hand and a Theban coin in the other, I could feel straight away that the Theban one was lighter. Mnasippos and Polykritos argued over the difference for a long time, in Boeotian so quick I lost half of it, and I walked away with a little pile of shields in my bag.

Based on evidence: About 520 BCE, according to the Archaeological Museum of Thebes, the league of the Boeotians was founded, and its member cities used a common coin with the Boeotian shield on one side. The Theban silver coin in the picture was struck about 500 to 480 BCE and weighs 12.10 grams. The Athenian four-drachma coin of about 500 BCE shown for day 1, in the Berlin Coin Cabinet, weighs 17 grams.
