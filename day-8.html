Day 8 · 24 November, 500 BCE

Plataea to Thebes

Damon walked me to his door before sunrise and gave me the name of a Theban he sells barley to, Polykritos, a merchant of Thebes. "Say that Damon of Plataea sent you," he told me, "and he'll feed you." I walked north for most of the morning across the fields of Plataea to the river Asopos, the border he'd shown me from the hill. The river was brown and fast after the week's rain. Eudoros, a goatherd of Plataea watering his goats at the bank, pointed out where the bottom was gravel instead of mud. I took off my sandals and led the donkey across the river. The water came up to my knees and was so cold my bones ached. On the far bank I stood dripping but excited, because for the first time in my life I was standing in the land of Thebes.

From the river to the city of Thebes I walked only about 5.5 km over gentle farmland. Halfway along I passed Potniai, a village with a grove of Demeter and Kore, where the wet leaves smelled sweet in the sun. In the early afternoon I climbed up to upper Thebes, on the Kadmeia, a hill above the plain, with my legs still damp. Every time I asked for directions, people noticed my Athenian accent and did a double take. Nobody's forgotten the war between Thebes and Athens, which was just six years ago.

Polykritos opened the door, and I told him that Damon of Plataea had sent me. Polykritos took the donkey's rope out of my hand, sat me by his fire with a cup of warm wine, and said that a friend of Damon's was welcome in his house, Athenian or not.

Based on evidence: Herodotus wrote that Athens made the river Asopos the border between Thebes and Plataea. From the crossing of the Asopos it was about 5.5 km to Potniai and the Kadmeia, the hill of upper Thebes, 200 metres above the sea. Potniai, about 3 km south of the Kadmeia, showed lively worship in the Archaic period. Pausanias, writing in the 100s CE, saw a grove of Demeter and Kore there. In 506 BCE the Boeotians captured Oinoe and Hysiai, on the border of Attica.

Sources
