Day 1 · 17 November, 500 BCE

Athens to Eleusis

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

The agora of Athens was still grey and cold when I got there this morning, and the stalls were empty. At the Altar of the Twelve Gods I poured a little wine on the stone and asked for a safe road. Athenians measure every road out of the city from that altar, so every step I take from now on counts from there. Standing at it, I felt the whole journey start, and I could hardly keep still.

Silver for the journey hangs in a leather bag under my tunic, in the form of a stack of owls, the heavy four-drachma coins with Athena's head on one side and her owl on the other. All morning I kept touching the bag through the cloth. Next to it are my benefactor's letters, each one asking a friend of his on the road to feed me and give me a bed. They're only a few request sheets, and the first will already be used tonight.

I walked for about 23 km from Athens to the town of Eleusis, through low hills of scrub and stone. Every autumn the procession of the Mysteries walks this same road, but today I had it almost to myself, with the donkey plodding behind under my blanket, food and writing things. In the late afternoon I came over the last rise and saw the bay of Eleusis below me. The island of Salamis was dark across the water. My first letter was for Philon, a barley farmer who lived in Eleusis as well as an old friend of my benefactor. Philon read it at his door by lamplight, smiled, and pulled me inside to the fire.
