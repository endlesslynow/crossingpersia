Day 2 · 18 November, 500 BCE

Eleusis

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

The soles of my feet were still burning this morning from yesterday's 23 km. Philon joked at the way I walked and took me slowly up through the town to the sanctuary of Demeter and Kore. Two months ago this hillside was packed with people who had walked from Athens for the Mysteries. This morning there were only a few women carrying small offerings, a priest sweeping the steps, and the wind coming in off the bay.

Inside the gate are painted clay plaques, gifts to the goddesses from people whose prayers were answered. On one of them a woman drives a chariot, holding the reins in a white arm, and Apollo sits beside her with his lyre. The colours were so bright they looked wet. I stood in front of it for a long time, wondering who had paid for it and what Demeter had done for them.

Photo: Fragment of a painted clay plaque of the 500s BCE, showing a woman in a chariot and Apollo with his lyre. Archaeological Museum of Eleusis, no. 385. Photographed in 2008. Photo Credit: Photo by Zde, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Based on evidence: The sanctuary of Demeter at Eleusis was on the eastern slope of the town's hill, in the middle of the town rather than on its edge. About 580 to 570 BCE its main terrace was enlarged, and the first hall of initiation was built on it soon after. The painted plaque in the picture was made in the 500s BCE and is kept in the Eleusis museum.
