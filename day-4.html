Day 4 · 20 November, 500 BCE

Oinoe

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

Rain drummed on the roof all day and turned the track outside to mud, so I stayed. Glaukos's wife and daughters sat by the open door sorting the olives brought in from the trees, picking out leaves and twigs. By noon their hands were black with olive juice, and the whole house smelled of wet earth, smoke and olives.

Lysis, the old father of Glaukos, sat with me by the fire and told me about the war. Oinoe has been one of the demes of Athens only since the reforms of Cleisthenes, eight years ago. Two years after that, a Boeotian army came over the mountain and took the village, while a Spartan army camped at Eleusis. Lysis said the Boeotians drove off the goats first, and then took everything else. In Athens we tell that year as the story of how we beat the Boeotians afterwards. Up here in Oinoe they still tell it as the year they lost the goats, and Lysis hasn't forgiven anyone.

Photo: Men beating olives off the trees, on a storage jar made in Athens about 520 BCE by the painter known as the Antimenes Painter. It was found at Vulci in Italy and is in the British Museum, London (1837,0609.42). Photographed in 2018. Photo Credit: Photo by ArchaiOptix, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Based on evidence: Oinoe became one of the demes of Athens after 508/7 BCE. In 506 BCE, while the Spartan king Kleomenes invaded Eleusis, the Boeotians captured Oinoe and Hysiai. Athens then beat the Boeotians and took 700 prisoners, ransomed at two minas each.
