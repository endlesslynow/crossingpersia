Day 6 · 22 November, 500 BCE

Hysiai to Plataea

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

From Hysiai to Plataea I walked only about 8 km west along the foot of Mount Cithaeron, and after yesterday it felt like a stroll. The sky had cleared, and the mountain above us was sharp and green. At the edge of Hysiai there's a small shrine of Demeter. I stopped and left a piece of bread for her, because four days ago I stood in her great sanctuary at Eleusis, and finding her here felt like meeting an old friend in a strange village.

The spring below Plataea is the biggest I've seen on this road, and half the town's animals seemed to be crowded round it. There were sheep, oxen and a string of mules, and boys shouting at all of them. The donkey shoved in among them and drank as if she'd walked from Athens for it. Above the spring, the town of Plataea is on a low hill. My second letter was for Damon, a landowner of Plataea and an old friend of my benefactor. Damon read it on his doorstep and took me in like family.

Photo: Mount Cithaeron seen from the village of Erythres, which is on the site of ancient Hysiai, photographed in June 2015. The trees and fields are modern. Photo Credit: Photo by Dafniotis, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Based on evidence: Herodotus wrote that Athens made the river Asopos the border between Thebes on one side and Plataea and Hysiai on the other. Plataea lay about 5 km west of Hysiai as the crow flies. Two inscriptions from the early 400s BCE recorded a cult image given to Demeter at Hysiai. A large spring at the foot of Plataea has been used since distant antiquity and is still used today for fields and livestock.
