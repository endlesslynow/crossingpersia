Day 10 · 26 November, 500 BCE

Thebes

◆ ◆ Map
دانلود صدا

The temple of Apollo Ismenios was on the Ismenian hill, just south-east of the city. Polykritos walked me up to it this morning. It was a Doric temple of stone. Inside, in the half dark, were the gifts that Croesus, king of the Lydians, sent for the hero Amphiaraos. There was a shield of solid gold and a spear made all of gold, the shaft as well as the head. All my life I've heard stories of Croesus's gold. Now I stood so close to it that I could see the lamp flames in the shield.

Three old tripods stood nearby with letters cut into them. Polykritos called them Kadmeian letters, but to my eye most of them looked like the letters the Ionians write. Being able to read, I bent down and spelled them out slowly, one by one. One said, "Skaios the boxer, in return for his victory, dedicated me, an ornament of beauty, for you, Apollo Far-shooter." Another said that Amphitryon gave it from the spoils he took from the Teleboai.

Back at the house, Theano, the wife of Polykritos, and Ismene, their older daughter, were kneading tomorrow's bread together at a long trough. Myrto, the younger daughter, played the flute beside them to keep them in time, and every push of their hands came on the beat. I sat on the step and watched them. The whole house smelled of flour and wood smoke. I wonder if I'll ever have a life like that.

Based on evidence: The temple of Ismenian Apollo stood on the Ismenian hill south-east of ancient Thebes. A Doric temple built in the Archaic period replaced the first one and stood until the early 300s BCE. Herodotus wrote that Croesus gave Amphiaraos a shield made entirely of gold and a spear of solid gold, and that both could still be seen in his day in the temple of Ismenian Apollo at Thebes. Herodotus also saw three tripods there engraved with "Kadmeian letters" that looked mostly like Ionian letters, and he quoted the words of Skaios the boxer and of Amphitryon. The figurine in the picture was made in Boeotia between 525 and 475 BCE and found at Thebes.
